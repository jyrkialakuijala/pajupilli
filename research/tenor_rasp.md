# The tenor's rasp: what is observed, and a checklist of 25 hypotheses

Begun 2026-11-28 on the author's ear ("tenor is a little raspy") and request ("let's try to observe it
and develop 25 hypothesis on the problem", "make a checklist of these hypothesis and start going through
it in priority order"). Measured on one song (the flute part of a jazz trio's file, 160 sung vowels), the
singer alone, no vibrato, unpractised; on the sounding part of each vowel (the stretch within 6 dB of its
loudest 10 ms).

## Observed

- The tenor's cycles are irregular: period change from one cycle to the next 2 to 6.5 % (the soprano's
  0.8 to 2 %), amplitude 4 to 13 % (2.5 to 4 %). The unclean energy lies at 100 to 1000 Hz, not in hiss.
- It is the note's beginning: on long notes 4.2 % in the first 20 to 80 ms, 1.8 % in the next 70, 1.4 %
  after. Baritone 6.1 %, mezzo 4.8 %, soprano 0.84 % in the first 80 ms.
- A short note is nearly all beginning: the worst tenth sound for 80 ms of a 190 ms slot.
- The tall mouth (Expr::tall) made the beginning worse: 2.7 % without, 4.2 % with.
- By vowel: "oo" and "ee" 1 to 2 %, "ah", "oh", "uh" 4 to 6 %, "ih" the worst.
- Under C4 the energy between the partials (period doubling) is about -17 dB; above, -29 dB.

## Checklist, in the order of testing

Marks: [ ] open, [x] tested (the result after it), [~] partly.

1. [x] H25 the yardstick itself favours a high voice. For the rasp: no. For the pitch miss: yes (below).
2. [~] H4 a voiced stop loses its voicing in the closure and the folds start again. Suggestive (below); few notes without a stop.
3. [x] H1 the folds need a number of cycles to settle, not a time. No: not ordered by pitch (below).
4. [ ] H3 the folds are still apart when the vowel begins.
5. [x] H11 the tall mouth puts the first resonance on a harmonic of his notes. No: 4.8 % without, 5.1 % with.
6. [x] H12 the tall mouth's longer lip tube sends a stronger wave back. No: 5.0 % with the tube not lengthened.
7. [~] H8 on "ah", "oh", "uh" the first resonance lies near his 2nd harmonic and pushes the folds. Supported by the vowels (below); not yet tested directly.
8. [x] H10 the shield (75 % under 5 kHz) is too little for heavier folds. No, the reverse: the shield was most of the rasp (below). Taken off.
9. [ ] H2 the breath pressure overshoots at the stop's release.
10. [x] H5 the stressed syllable's scoop moves the tension while the folds start. No: nothing changes without it.
11. [ ] H6 the closure held 30 % longer on weighted syllables builds too much pressure.
12. [ ] H7 pitch and tension still glide when the voicing starts (targets arrive late).
13. [ ] H9 the tract moves fastest right after the consonant, sweeping a resonance over a harmonic.
14. [x] H13 the head resonances' mouth opening (0.1) adds to it. No: 4.7 % without, within the scatter.
15. [x] H14 the tongue-forward gesture is partly on for his top notes. No: it begins at C5, above his range; the rendering is the same.
16. [~] H15 most of the song lies across his register change (the knee at D4). Supported: the knee lower is smoother, higher much rougher (below).
17. [ ] H16 under C4 the folds double their period: too slack for the pressure.
18. [ ] H17 his learnt tension map has rough places.
19. [~] H18 the folds are not a tenor's. Their mass matters: lighter is smoother (below). Closeness not tested yet.
20. [ ] H19 irregularity carried over from the speech settings is too strong for singing.
21. [x] H20 the random drift is too much for a low voice. No: the same without it.
22. [~] H21 the level hold pumps at the beginnings. Together with H22 and H23: they colour the beginning as measured (below); not separated yet.
23. [~] H22 the resonances on partials 3 and 5 chase a gliding pitch at the beginning. See H21.
24. [~] H23 the semitone resonators ring on from the note before. See H21.
25. [ ] H24 no steady vowel at all: the next consonant begins too early (80 ms of 190 sound).

## Results

Columns: the vowels' harmonic share, median and worst tenth (dB); the period's change from a cycle to the next
in the first 60 ms of the sound and after (%); the amplitude's (%).

**H25.** A made tone, clean and in tune, with the song's note lengths: 37 dB and 0.3 to 0.7 % at a tenor's
pitch, 46 dB and 0.4 to 1.1 % at a soprano's. The tenor's 17 dB and 5 % are far from that floor; the
soprano's 0.84 % at the beginning is the floor. But the same tone "misses" its pitch by 9.2 cents (median) at
the tenor's pitch and 3.3 at the soprano's: the measure takes the strongest bin of a short window without
interpolating. The low voices' pitch miss in earlier tables is partly that.

**H10, reversed.** The shield makes the tenor rougher, evenly from none to 0.97 (the first table's 0.90 and
0.97). It was made for the soprano's light folds.

**A 26th, the ear** (the pitch corrected by a share of the error every cycle, itself a change from cycle to
cycle): no. Without it he is rougher and far out of tune; at half the share the same as now.

**H15, H18.** Lighter folds and a lower knee are both smoother, heavier and higher both rougher. The tension
map was learnt with the folds as they are, so these renderings sing on a map that is not theirs: a trend, not
yet a setting.

## What was done (2026-11-28)

- **The tenor's folds:** mass 0.8 -> 0.6, the register change 294 / 392 -> 235 / 314 Hz, his ear at half
  (the author: "try reduce fold mass, perhaps 0.75 is ok ... register knee ... do it, and use pitch controller
  at half"). Unpractised on the song: the period's change 3.95 -> 3.08 % (after the first 60 ms 2.98 -> 2.13 %),
  clean 17.1 -> 18.0 dB, its worst tenth 11.4 -> 13.3 dB. The first 60 ms unchanged, 5.1 %.
- **The shield by voice** (the period's change, all of the vowel; clean median / worst tenth, dB):

| voice | shield 0.75 | 0.40 | none |
|---|---|---|---|
| bass | 4.2 %, 18.7 / 11.3 | | 3.6 %, 18.2 / 11.9 |
| baritone | 4.3 %, 17.9 / 11.7 | | 3.4 %, 17.7 / 12.1 |
| tenor, the new folds | 3.1 %, 18.0 / 13.3 | 2.3 %, 17.7 / 11.1 | 2.0 %, 15.2 / 8.6 |
| countertenor | 2.2 %, 18.4 / 12.4 | 1.8 %, 18.6 / 11.6 | 1.7 %, 17.9 / 10.5 |
| contralto | 2.7 %, 20.0 / 13.7 | 2.3 %, 20.6 / 11.3 | 1.9 %, 20.5 / 12.7 |
| mezzo | 2.8 %, 21.2 / 11.9 | 2.0 %, 21.3 / 11.6 | 1.9 %, 21.7 / 12.3 |
| soprano | 1.0 %, 21.9 / 14.7 | | 1.4 %, 17.3 / -7.2 |
| coloratura | 0.9 %, 21.2 / 13.5 | | 1.0 %, 17.8 / -2.8 |

  The author, after listening: "Bass, Baritone, Tenor, Alto, Mezzo better without shield, turn shield to 0 %",
  and: "tenor sounds much better without shield". None for those five; 0.75 kept for the soprano and the
  coloratura (their worst notes break without it), and for the countertenor and the profondo, not yet decided
  (the profondo not measured: the yardstick fails at his pitch).
- **A lesson on the yardstick:** for the tenor without the shield the worst tenth of the harmonic share fell
  (13.3 -> 8.6 dB) while the cycles grew more regular, and the ear chose the regular cycles. The harmonic share's
  worst tenth alone is not to be trusted for such a choice.
- **"ah" in every vowel** (PJ_SINGER_AH 0.2, the author's wish of the same day) costs the tenor what the folds
  gained in regularity (3.08 -> 3.95 %); the worst tenth 13.3 -> 11.8 dB. Measured before the shield came off.
